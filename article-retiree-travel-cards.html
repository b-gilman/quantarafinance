Best travel credit cards for
Canadian retirees in 2026

Travel insurance, lounge access, and flexible redemptions — what retirees actually need from a travel card, and which Canadian cards deliver it.

Most travel credit card guides are written for frequent business travellers in their 30s and 40s. They focus on points accumulation, airline status, and maximizing spend on corporate expenses. That's not the retiree travel experience.

Retirees travel differently. Longer trips, often international. More concern about medical emergencies abroad. Fixed income that makes annual fees a real consideration. A preference for simplicity over complex points programs. And often, a loyalty to Air Canada that's been built over decades of Canadian life.

We reviewed 22 travel-eligible cards with retirees specifically in mind. Here's what actually matters — and which cards deliver it.

What retirees need from a travel card

Emergency medical insurance (especially coverage for travellers over 65), trip cancellation and interruption coverage, no foreign transaction fees, flexible redemptions with no blackout dates, and airport lounge access for longer layovers. Reward rate matters less than insurance quality at this life stage.

The most important feature most people overlook: medical insurance age limits

The single biggest differentiator among travel cards for retirees is emergency medical insurance — specifically, the age cutoff and the number of covered days per trip. Most standard travel cards cap emergency medical coverage at age 65, or dramatically reduce the covered days for travellers over 65.

A card that covers a 45-year-old for unlimited trip days may only cover a 70-year-old for 3–7 days per trip. For a retiree spending six weeks in Europe or a month in Florida, that gap is the difference between a credit card perk and genuine peace of mind.

"The best travel card for a retiree isn't the one with the most points. It's the one that covers you when you're 3,000 kilometres from home and something goes wrong."

1. Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite — Best overall for retirees

The Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite is our top pick for most Canadian retirees. It combines no foreign transaction fees, six free airport lounge visits per year via Visa Airport Companion, strong Scene+ rewards on travel and dining, and comprehensive travel insurance — all at a $150 annual fee that's genuinely justified by the benefits.

The no foreign transaction fee benefit alone can save a retiree who travels internationally $150–300 per year — effectively paying for the card. Pair that with the lounge access (invaluable for longer connections) and it becomes one of the best value propositions in Canadian travel cards.

Who should get the Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite?

Retirees who travel internationally 1–3 times per year, make purchases in foreign currencies, and want lounge access for longer connections. The no foreign transaction fee benefit is the strongest single feature for international travellers at any age.

2. TD First Class Travel Visa Infinite — Best for extended trips over 65

The TD First Class Travel Visa Infinite stands out specifically for retirees over 65 because of its extended emergency medical coverage. While most cards cap coverage at 15 days per trip for travellers over 65, TD offers coverage for longer trips — critical for the month-long Florida winters or European spring trips that define Canadian retirement travel.

It also offers up to $500 in annual travel credits redeemable through Expedia for TD, and strong TD Rewards earn rates on travel purchases booked through the TD portal.

What to look for — the retiree travel card checklist

  • Emergency medical insurance age limit: Confirm whether coverage applies past age 65 and how many days per trip are covered. Read the certificate of insurance, not just the marketing page.
  • No foreign transaction fees: Standard cards charge 2.5% on every foreign currency purchase. On a $5,000 international trip, that's $125 in avoidable fees.
  • Trip cancellation and interruption: Covers non-refundable costs if you have to cancel or cut a trip short due to illness or family emergency.
  • Lounge access: Long connections are more common on international itineraries. Even 2–4 free visits per year adds meaningful comfort.
  • Flexible redemptions: Avoid programs that lock you into one airline or require booking through a specific portal. Flexibility matters when your schedule is your own.
Important: always read the insurance certificate

Credit card travel insurance is a genuine benefit — but the details matter enormously. Pre-existing condition exclusions, trip length limits, and age cutoffs vary significantly between cards. Download the full certificate of insurance (not the summary) and read it before relying on card insurance for a major trip.

The bottom line

For most Canadian retirees, the Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite is the strongest all-around option — the no foreign transaction fee benefit alone justifies the $150 annual fee for anyone who travels internationally. If you're over 65 and take longer trips, the TD First Class Travel Visa Infinite's extended medical coverage is worth the close look. And if you want to earn heavily on everyday spending year-round, the Scotia Gold Amex is the most rewarding card in the category.
